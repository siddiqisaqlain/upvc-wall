## 1. Materials and finishes

- **SS 304 vs SS 202.** 304 has about 18–20% chromium and 8–10.5% nickel. 202 has about 17–19% chromium, 4–6% nickel and 7.5–10% manganese, so it is the cheaper, manganese-rich grade. — https://www.chefpicks.in/guides/stainless-steel-grades-304-vs-202/
- **Why 202 rusts.** Most 200-series grades have corrosion resistance close to ferritic grade 430, which is lower than 304, because they contain less chromium. They are fine indoors at room temperature but are not recommended where chlorides are present (coastal air, cleaning chemicals, wet areas). — https://www.assda.asn.au/blog/262-200-series-stainless-steels-high-manganese-crmn and https://aalco.co.uk/datasheets/Stainless-Steel-201201L202204_97.ashx
- **The magnet test does not work.** Both 304 and 202 are austenitic and essentially non-magnetic, so a magnet cannot tell them apart. Cold-working can make 304 slightly magnetic. A strong pull suggests a 400-series steel or a plated mild-steel part, not 304. To confirm the grade you need a manganese drop-test kit, an XRF analyser or a mill test certificate, plus a grade stamp on the product. — https://www.chefpicks.in/guides/stainless-steel-grades-304-vs-202/ and https://finishing.com/441/88.shtml
- Some 200-series steel has been deliberately mislabelled as 304. — https://aalco.co.uk/datasheets/Stainless-Steel-201201L202204_97.ashx
- **Grade 316** (marine grade) contains molybdenum and is the usual choice for coastal or exterior doors. — https://www.morehandles.co.uk/blog/Technical-Guides-I-live-by-the-sea-How-can-I-prevent-salt-spray-marine-corrosion/
- **Zinc alloy (Zamak / "ZAMA").** Cheap and die-cast, and always plated with chrome, nickel or brass. It is softer underneath and corrodes once the plating wears through. Brass versions of the same product typically cost about 30–80% more than Zamak ones. — https://www.morehandles.co.uk/blog/More-Handles-Technical-Guides-Solid-Brass-Vs-Zamak-Does-base-metal-material-make-any-difference-when-choosing-door-handles
- **Brass** resists corrosion well. Avoid lacquered brass near salt air. — https://door.watersonusa.com/knowledge/technical/material-comparison and https://www.morehandles.co.uk/blog/Technical-Guides-I-live-by-the-sea-How-can-I-prevent-salt-spray-marine-corrosion/
- **Mixed metals.** A stainless hinge on a plain steel frame forms a galvanic cell and speeds up corrosion. — https://door.watersonusa.com/knowledge/technical/material-comparison
- **PVD vs electroplating.** Electroplating lays a metal layer from a chemical bath. PVD (physical vapour deposition) condenses metal compounds in a vacuum and gives a harder, more scratch- and fade-resistant layer. PVD is how gold, rose-gold and black finishes are made durable. Electroplating is cheaper and suits classic polished or antique looks. — https://www.griffwerk.de/en/blog/surface-quality-of-door-handles/ and https://lemgalvanica.com/en/pvd-electroplating-differences-advantages-limitations/
- Rose gold is only as durable as its coating, so ask for **PVD** rose gold. — https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/door-hardware-finishes-india

## 2. Hinges

- **Butt hinge.** Steel types under IS 1341 come in light, medium and heavy weight, with equal or unequal flaps. — https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/bisconnect/knowyourstandards/Indian_standards/isdetails/MjU1ODE= and https://alephindia.in/isi-product/steel-butt-hinges.php
- **How many hinges per door (rule of thumb).** Up to 1524 mm (60 in) high, use 2 hinges. From 1524 to 2286 mm (60–90 in), use 3. Add one more for every further 762 mm (30 in). So a typical 2100 mm Indian door takes 3 hinges. Heavy and fire-rated doors follow the manufacturer's load rating. — https://hagerco.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/73000552399-determine-the-size-of-hinge and https://www.assaabloy.com/ae/en/content-fragments/products/doors-and-door-hardware/ansi-hardware-range/door-hinges/document/ANSI_Door_Hinges_General_Information_ME.pdf
- **Ball-bearing butt hinges** (bearings between the knuckles) are the first choice for heavy and high-use doors. — https://www.restorationonline.com.au/blog/door-hinge-guide/
- **Concealed ("auto", cup) hinge.** It has a 35 mm cup drilled into the shutter. It is sold in **full overlay**, **half overlay** and **inset** versions, usually with a 110° opening and an optional integrated soft-close ("Silent System"). Overlay and depth can be adjusted after fitting. — https://web.hettich.com/en-in/eshop-02-09-in-en and https://shop.hettich.com/gb_EN/-/Sensys-110%C2%B0-hinge-without-self-closing-feature/bp/variant12521024827821
- Indian shops name the overlay by its **"crank"**. Listings show a **0 crank** soft-close hinge (Häfele), an **8 crank** half-overlay hinge (Ebco E-HSM2) and a **16 crank** inset hinge (Ebco HSM3, in SS 304). — https://mccoymart.com/buy/hafele-soft-close-concealed-hinge-with-0-crank-nickel-plated , https://mccoymart.com/buy/ebco-e-hsm2-euro-half-overlay-slow-motion-concealed-hinge-with-8-crank-and-2-hole-mounting-plate-nickel-plated and https://mccoymart.com/buy/ebco-hsm3-m1-ss-inset-16-crank-slow-motion-concealed-hinge-with-4-hole-mounting-plate-stainless-steel-304
- **Hinge count for cabinet doors** depends mainly on the door's weight, then its height. Blum's charts assume a 600 mm wide door. Space the hinges as far apart as possible. — https://ea.blum.com/en/number-of-hinges/
- **Piano (continuous) hinge.** A full-length hinge for box lids, fall-flaps and fold-down tables (IS 3818). **Parliament hinge.** Its projecting knuckle lets a shutter swing 180° clear of an architrave or deep reveal (IS 362). **Tee and strap hinges** are for battened doors and gates (IS 206). — BIS lists above
- **Floor spring and pivot.** A floor spring is a hydraulic closer buried in the floor under a pivoted door. It is used for heavy timber and frameless glass doors and can be single- or double-action, with or without hold-open (IS 6315). Example: the dormakaba BTS 75 V suits single- and double-action doors up to 1100 mm wide, with closing force EN 1–4. — https://www.dormakaba.com/in-en/offering/products/door-hardware/door-closers/bts-75-v--do_293
- **Spring hinges.** Double-acting spring hinges (IS 453) suit café, saloon and kitchen swing doors. — BIS list above

## 3. Bolts and latches

- **Tower bolt.** A surface-mounted sliding bolt fixed on the inside face of doors and windows (IS 204; stainless steel IS 15833).
- **Aldrop.** A heavy sliding bolt designed to take a **padlock**. It is used on main doors, store rooms and gates so the door can be locked from outside. The BIS title is "sliding door bolts (aldrops) for use with padlocks" (IS 2681 non-ferrous, IS 281 mild steel, IS 15834 stainless). It is also called a *kunda*. — BIS lists above and https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/door-locks-types-india
- **Hasp and staple** (IS 363), **flush bolt** (IS 5187, recessed into the edge of the passive leaf of a double door), **bathroom latch** (IS 5899) and **indicating bolt** (IS 4621, shows "vacant/engaged" on public toilets). — BIS list above
- Indicative price: aldrops are commonly 6–10 in long, with heavy sets roughly ₹150–600 (guide estimate, 2026). — https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/door-locks-types-india

## 4. Door locks and handles

- **Mortise lock.** The lock body is set into a pocket cut in the door edge. **Backset** is the distance from the door edge to the cylinder or spindle centre (commonly 45, 55, 60 or 65 mm). **Centre distance** is the spacing between the handle spindle and the keyhole or cylinder: 72 mm is the usual figure for interior and entrance doors, and 85 mm is common on narrow-stile and external locks. The euro-profile cylinder is bought separately. — https://www.geze.com.cn/en/products-solutions/p_91451 , https://goodio.be/en/product/354427-insteekslot-voor-cilinder-72-55-8-l-iseo and https://www.assaabloy.com/in/en/solutions/products/mechanical-test/backup/mechanical-lock/narrow-profile/aa5104-2085-aa5104-2585-aa5104-3085-aa5104-3585
- **Standards for handles.** Door handles are covered by IS 208, and handles for mortice locks (vertical type) by IS 4992. — BIS lists above and https://services.bis.gov.in/tmp/SR4992.pdf
- **Lever on plate vs on rose** (general description, no single source found). A plate handle has a long backplate that also carries the keyhole. A rose handle sits on a small round rose, and the keyhole has its own escutcheon.
- **Cylindrical/tubular (knob) lockset.** A bored lock with a pin-tumbler cylinder in the knob (IS 16016). **Rim lock / night latch.** Surface-mounted on the inside face (IS 1019). **Padlock.** IS 15275.
- **Digital locks** combine several access modes. Example: the Godrej Catus Touch Plus offers PIN (4–9 digits, up to 99), 360° fingerprint (up to 99), RFID card (up to 99) and a mechanical key for emergency override. It fits doors 35–65 mm thick, and an external power bank on its micro-USB port powers it if the batteries die. — https://www.godrejenterprises.com/locks-and-security/locks/digital-locks/p/catus-touch-plus-black
- Other models use an external **9 V battery** on contacts outside the lock as emergency power. Some locks have a "privacy mode" that blocks outside access, and an alarm after 5 wrong attempts. — https://godrejenterprises.com/Resources/Support/DigitalLocksCatalogue_04010229.pdf
- **Aligarh** (UP), the "Tala Nagari" or city of locks, got a GI tag for its locks in 2023. — https://nabard.org/gi/GIProductDetails.aspx?Id=15 and https://handicrafts.nic.in/crafts/All_Crafts/Craft_Categories/Metal/Other_Metal/Aligarh_Tala/AligarhTalaWebPage.html
- **Godrej** started making locks in Mumbai in **1897**, and its springless lock was patented in 1908. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardeshir_Godrej

## 11. Checklist for specifying hardware

1. **Door weight and size first.** Use 3 hinges up to 2286 mm height and add one per extra 762 mm. Use ball-bearing hinges on heavy or busy doors. Size the closer to the larger of its width and weight classes (EN 1154). — Hager and EN 1154 sources above
2. **Fire doors.** Use an EN 3 or larger closer, and follow the door maker's tested hardware set rather than a general rule. — https://safelincs.co.uk/what-is-the-en-power-size-of-a-fire-door-closer and https://idighardware.com/?p=23617
3. **Wet, coastal or exterior areas.** Use SS 304 at minimum and 316 near the sea. Avoid 202, plated Zamak and lacquered brass, and don't mix stainless hinges with plain-steel frames. — sections 1 and 2
4. **Ask for the ISI mark** on items under a Quality Control Order (for example steel butt hinges, IS 1341), and for a grade stamp or test certificate on "SS 304". — section 0
5. **Ergonomics and accessibility.** Prefer lever handles to knobs. India's *Harmonised Guidelines and Standards for Universal Accessibility 2021* (CPWD/MoHUA) set door and hardware requirements; check the exact heights in the document. — https://divyangjan.depwd.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/2262.pdf
6. **Match the lock to the door.** Check the backset, centre distance, door thickness (for example 35–65 mm for many digital locks) and handing (direction of opening) before ordering. — section 4
7. **Keep finishes consistent** across a project (one finish family, ideally PVD for coloured finishes), and specify the base metal as well as the finish. — section 1
8. **Cabinet fittings.** Choose the hinge crank (full / half overlay / inset) to suit the carcass, and choose the drawer runner by load (35–45 kg channels, 40–70 kg under-mount) and by length to suit the carcass depth. — section 6
